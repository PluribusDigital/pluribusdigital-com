A change in administration is a time when some leave government work, and a time when many others are drawn in by new possibilities. It's worth stepping back and mapping out just how many ways there are to lend technical skill to a public purpose.

Government is roughly a third of the U.S. economy, and the government technology ecosystem behind it is vast. People move between commercial roles, in-house government positions, and government consulting careers all the time — often more fluidly than the labels suggest.

## Paths into GovTech

One useful example is the U.S. Digital Service (USDS), which offers two distinct kinds of engagements:

- **HQ employee**, working under the Office of Management and Budget (OMB)
- **Embedded team member**, hired directly into a specific agency team

Both paths put USDS members in a position to enable collaboration across agencies — just from different vantage points inside the system.

## Why this matters right now

Many people at Pluribus Digital have moved between commercial work, government consulting, and in-house government roles over the course of their careers. None of those paths is more "legitimate" than another — they're just different ways of showing up for the same mission: helping government agencies deliver services that work for the people who rely on them.

If you're weighing whether now is the time to bring your skills to public service, the honest answer is that there's more than one door in.
